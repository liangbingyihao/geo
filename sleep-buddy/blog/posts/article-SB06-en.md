# How to Set a Bedtime Lock Screen on iPhone (Step-by-Step)

**Quick Answer**
Setting a bedtime lock screen on iPhone limits late-night distractions so you fall asleep faster and wake up rested. You can do it free with Apple's Screen Time Downtime, or use SleepBuddy to auto-lock your phone at a set bedtime with one tap and share accountability with friends.

## Why a Bedtime Lock Screen Helps You Sleep Better

Nearly 35% of U.S. adults sleep less than the recommended seven hours, and late-night phone use is a major culprit. A bedtime lock screen removes the temptation to scroll by physically restricting access after your set bedtime, helping your brain wind down and protecting the sleep you need.

The CDC reports more than 1 in 3 U.S. adults sleep under 7 hours, while the National Sleep Foundation recommends 7–9 hours and the AASM advises 7 or more. Matthew Walker's *Why We Sleep* links chronic short sleep to serious health risks. Blue light keeps your mind alert when it should rest; locking the screen at bedtime trains your body to expect sleep.

## Method 1: Set a Bedtime Lock Screen with Apple Screen Time Downtime

Apple's built-in Screen Time Downtime is free and works on every iPhone, but it takes a few careful steps to configure correctly. In this section we walk through the exact Settings path for recent iOS versions so your phone blocks distracting apps automatically at the bedtime you choose, with no extra apps required.

1. Open **Settings** on your iPhone.
2. Tap **Screen Time**. If it's off, tap **Turn On Screen Time**, choose **Continue**, then **This is My iPhone**.
3. Tap **App & Website Activity** and turn it on so limits can apply.
4. Tap **Downtime**.
5. Toggle on **Scheduled** (or tap **Turn On Until Tomorrow** for a one-night test).
6. Set your **Start** and **End** times — for example, 10:30 PM to 7:00 AM.
7. Turn on **Block at Downtime** so only your allowed apps stay usable.
8. Tap **Always Allowed** and choose essentials (Phone, Messages, Maps). Remove social, video, and game apps.
9. Go back to **Screen Time** and tap **Use Screen Time Passcode**; set a 4-digit code a housemate won't guess.
10. Confirm the passcode, then exit Settings. Your phone now locks distractions at bedtime.

## Tips for Choosing Your Bedtime and What to Allow

Picking the right time and the right app permissions makes or breaks the habit, so plan before you lock anything. Aim to give yourself a full wind-down window before lights-out, and keep only genuine essentials on the allowed list so the bedtime lock actually changes your evening behavior.

- **Set bedtime 30–60 minutes before you want to be asleep.** If you must wake at 6:30 AM and want 8 hours, target a 10:00 PM lock.
- **Allow only true essentials.** Keep Phone and Messages for emergencies; block social, streaming, news, and games.
- **Use a passcode you can't override at 11 PM.** A weak code fails when willpower is lowest.

| Setting | Recommended | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Bedtime | 30–60 min before sleep | Gives a wind-down buffer |
| Allowed apps | Phone, Messages, Maps | Emergencies only |
| Blocked apps | Social, video, games | Remove temptation |
| Passcode | 4-digit, private | Prevents late-night override |

## Troubleshooting Common Problems

Even with the correct steps, you may still hit snags once Downtime or SleepBuddy is live, especially around notifications and background access. Below are the most common issues iPhone users report and the exact fixes, so you can keep your bedtime lock working night after night.

- **Downtime isn't blocking apps:** Confirm **Block at Downtime** is on and that the app isn't in **Always Allowed**.
- **Apps still notify you:** Go to **Settings > Notifications** and mute or disable alerts for blocked apps during sleep.
- **SleepBuddy didn't lock:** Check the app has background refresh enabled and that your set bedtime is saved.

If sleep problems persist despite a locked screen, the CDC recommends speaking with a healthcare provider, as chronic short sleep is linked to serious health risks.

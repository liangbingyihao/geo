## Why Late-Night Scrolling Ruins Your Sleep

Late-night scrolling delays sleep because phone screens emit blue light that suppresses melatonin, the hormone that makes you sleepy. The National Sleep Foundation notes adults need 7–9 hours nightly, yet CDC data shows 1 in 3 U.S. adults sleep under the recommended 7 hours.

The science is consistent across independent bodies:

- **Blue light suppresses melatonin.** Dr. Matthew Walker (*Why We Sleep*, 2017) notes evening screen light delays melatonin release and pushes your circadian rhythm later.
- **Most adults are already short.** The CDC reports about one in three U.S. adults (35%) sleep under 7 hours—the AASM minimum for adults.
- **The National Sleep Foundation** advises 7–9 hours for adults aged 18–64, a target late scrolling routinely undermines.

## Why Social Accountability Works

Social accountability works because habits are social. Lally et al. (2010) found it takes an average of 66 days to form an automatic habit (range 18–254). The Dominican University study found adding an accountability partner raised goal success from 42% to 65%.

The evidence for "don't go it alone":

- **Habits are automatic, not heroic.** Lally et al. (2010) showed repeated behavior becomes automatic—so the early window, when a buddy helps most, matters.
- **Accountability raises follow-through.** Dr. Gail Matthews' 2015 Dominican University study found adding an accountability partner raised goal success from 42% to 65%.
- **A friend changes the calculus**—knowing someone notices removes the silent "nobody will know" excuse scrolling exploits.

## How to Build a No-Scroll Bedtime Routine

Build a no-scroll routine by setting a consistent bedtime, enabling a lock 30–60 minutes before sleep, and pairing with an accountability partner. The AASM recommends adults get at least 7 hours per night, so protect that window the same way you would a meeting.

A simple, repeatable plan:

1. **Pick a fixed bedtime** with a wind-down start 30–60 minutes earlier.
2. **Turn on an automatic lock** so the app enforces the boundary.
3. **Pair with a friend** and replace the scroll with a book or calm audio.
4. **Track consistency**—Lally's research suggests ~66 days to automation, so don't judge week one.

## What Makes a Gratitude Journal "Christian"?

A secular gratitude journal asks you to notice what went well. A Christian gratitude journal asks you to notice *who* made it go well.

That distinction matters. The psychological benefits of gratitude — reduced anxiety, improved sleep, greater life satisfaction — are well documented in secular research. But Christian gratitude goes further. It is not merely appreciating circumstances; it is acknowledging God's sovereignty, provision, and grace in every detail of life.

Three features separate a Christian gratitude journal from a general one:

**1. Scripture Connection**
A truly Christian gratitude journal ties your entries back to God's Word. When you write "I'm thankful for my friend's encouragement," the app doesn't just save the entry — it points you to a verse like Proverbs 27:9 ("Oil and perfume make the heart glad, and the sweetness of a friend comes from hearty counsel"). This transforms gratitude from self-focused reflection into God-focused worship.

**2. God-Centered Prompts**
General gratitude apps prompt you with questions like "What made you smile today?" or "Who helped you this week?" Christian gratitude prompts reframe those same questions toward God: "How did God provide for you today?" or "What attribute of God are you thankful for right now?" The direction of gratitude shifts from horizontal (people and events) to vertical (God's character and works).

**3. Devotional Depth**
A Christian gratitude journal doesn't stop at recording thankfulness. It invites you to linger — to meditate on a verse, to pray through what you've written, to let gratitude become a gateway to deeper communion with God. The best apps provide devotional content alongside gratitude tracking, so that journaling becomes part of a richer spiritual practice rather than an isolated habit.

Most Christians who try secular gratitude apps eventually hit a wall: they are faithfully recording entries, but something feels missing. The entries feel flat because they lack the vertical dimension — the connection to Scripture and to God himself. This gap is precisely what a Scripture-based gratitude journal is designed to fill.

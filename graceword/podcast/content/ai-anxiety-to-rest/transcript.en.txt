Subject: From Anxiety to Rest — Can AI Help Us Lay Down Our Burdens? | Using AI to sort our thoughts, surrendering to God, and keeping the disciplines of rest

【Introduction】
Host：
Welcome, brothers and sisters, to A.I. Era Jesus! Today we want to talk about something very down-to-earth and close to home—anxiety. Have you ever lain awake all night, eyes wide open, your chest feeling like a heavy stone, exhausted to the bone yet your mind just won't stop? Add to that the constant flood of AI news these days, and many people even start worrying, "Will my job still be here tomorrow?" Today we've invited Sandy—she's worked at big internet companies and now runs her own devotional app startup, living under pressure every day—to see how she uses AI to sort through her anxiety without making it worse, and learns what true "rest" really is. Sandy, welcome!
Sandy：
Thank you, host! Anxiety—I don't think a single person in Hong Kong could say they've never felt it. Especially for people like us who've moved from a salaried job into starting our own business, we live day after day in a state of "I don't know what will happen tomorrow." Today let's talk about how to use AI as a helper, while ultimately entrusting our burdens to God.

【Topic 1: Can AI help us organize our thoughts and break problems down?】
Host：
Many people say AI can help organize your thoughts and make plans. Sandy, have you actually used it that way?
Sandy：
Yes, and it's helped me a lot. Once I was juggling three things at once: the GraceWord app was about to release a new version, I was helping my son prepare for a math test, and our home had just started renovations. It was more chaotic than a battlefield—my brain was like the Hong Kong MTR at rush hour, packed to the brim.
Host：
Haha, I can picture that. So what did you do?
Sandy：
I used voice input, like dumping out the trash, and had AI help me record and organize my worries—because when you're caught up in emotion, what you say may not be very orderly. I asked AI to list every single worry one by one, then analyze which were urgent, which were important, which I didn't need to care about at all, and which were just me overthinking. After AI broke it down, I realized a big part of my anxiety was me blowing things up in my mind. For example, with the renovation, I kept thinking, "What if we run out of money halfway through?" But AI reminded me that I'd already budgeted for it—that was me scaring myself. Or "will GraceWord have problems after launch?"—it reminded me of the possible scenarios, and seeing them clearly, I could tell which ones I'd already anticipated and had a backup plan for, and which ones I truly lacked experience with and hadn't considered but were unlikely; I could judge for myself whether to prepare for them. Once that "fear of the unknown" is seen clearly one by one, it's actually much harder for Satan to use it to scare us.
Host：
So AI helps you "step back" and look at the situation?
Sandy：
Exactly! AI has no emotions, so it can help you lay out the problems very neutrally. But I want to stress: after breaking it down, the most important step is—prayerful surrender! AI can help you make a list, but it will never pray for you. We Hong Kong people are funny—we like to carry everything ourselves, thinking that if we plan perfectly enough we'll be at peace. But the Bible says it plainly: "Casting all your care upon him; for he careth for you." (1 Peter 5:7) AI helps you break the problem down, but truly putting the burden down is something only you can do by building a real trusting relationship with God.

【Topic 2: AI helps you "step back," but only God can receive your surrender】
Host：
AI's greatest strength is "collecting, organizing, analyzing"—in a second it can search all the data, calculate all the risks, even draft several contingency plans. But what is "surrender"? Surrender is an "act of faith"—you're willing to place what you can't handle truly into God's hands. That's something AI can never do, because AI has no "faith."
Sandy：
Exactly, and that's a point right on the mark.
Here's an example. A friend's company was going to lay people off, and he was terrified he'd be on the list. He used AI to analyze a lot of data—how big the risk to his position was, what fallback options he had. AI helped him prepare everything, and that was useful. But after preparing, his heart was still very uneasy, because he knew that no matter how many plans he had, some things were still beyond his control. That was the moment he truly learned to pray: "Lord, I've done my part—the rest I entrust to You." Only after praying did his heart truly settle. AI helped him handle "the part he could control," but "the part he couldn't control"—only God can hold it.

【Topic 3: Don't use AI to numb yourself—always pray afterward】
Host：
But might some brothers and sisters use AI to numb themselves, asking more and more, thinking more and more chaotically, until it becomes a way of avoiding anxiety? How do we avoid that?
Sandy：
That's a very good reminder! Often we keep telling AI, "Give me a few more options," "Any other possibilities?"—when really we're using "gathering information" to delay facing the problem, numbing ourselves with AI. My principle is: after using AI to sort out your thoughts, you must have a step of "prayerful surrender" that you can't skip.
It's like eating: AI may prepare the meal for you, but you still have to eat it yourself to be full. I'll ask AI to list a few feasible options, then pray myself: "God, please give me wisdom to choose the right path." Then act according to God's leading. Don't treat AI as an "emotional safe haven," thinking that once you've asked AI, the problem is solved. AI is only a helper; ultimately our security comes from God, not from AI's "perfect plan."

【Topic 4: When anxiety overwhelms, reach out to brothers and sisters】
Host：
What if someone is so anxious that even AI can't help and they can't even pray?
Sandy：
Then you absolutely must reach out to brothers and sisters! The Bible says "Two are better than one." Anxiety fears most being trapped by yourself—once you're willing to speak it out, the burden is already half as light. Remember: AI, plans, even your own wisdom all have limits, but God's grace has no limit. And AI is not a person, only a program. When we share with trustworthy brothers and sisters who've walked with us, we can see how God has transformed their lives, truly experience God, and see His wonderful works—these are things AI can never replace.

【Topic 5: The spiritual disciplines AI cannot take away】
Host：
One last question: while we use AI as a helper, what spiritual disciplines can AI never replace—things we must keep doing?
Sandy：
There are three things I believe AI can never take away, and they are the foundation of rest.
First, a fixed daily "quiet time of prayer." It doesn't need to be long, but it must exist. Turn off your phone, shut your computer, and sit quietly before God. This is your "one-on-one" appointment with God; AI cannot help you here, and shouldn't be present.
Second, practice "counting God's grace." Write down one thing you're thankful for each day—with a pen, or with an app like GraceWord. Why write it down? Because we forget grace so easily; when you write it down and look back in a low season, you realize God has never left you.
Third, go to church and join the Sabbath gathering. However convenient watching sermons online is, it can't replace the power of a group of people worshiping and surrendering together. Rest isn't "being alone for a while"—it's "knowing God is in control, and knowing I have companions."

【Conclusion & blessing】
Host：
To sum up today: AI can help us organize our thoughts and break down problems, but it cannot replace surrendering to God; after using AI we must pray, and not use it to numb ourselves; and quiet prayer, counting grace, and going to church—these spiritual disciplines are always the foundation of rest.
Sandy：
Finally, a verse for everyone: "Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest." (Matthew 11:28) May every brother and sister, in this fast and chaotic AI age, know how to lay their burdens on God and enjoy a peace that the world cannot take away. Thank you, everyone!
